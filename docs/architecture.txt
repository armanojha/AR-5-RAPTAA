DRISHTI IMPLEMENTATION ARCHITECTURE

1. Competition position

Primary submission: Track A, Read the Planet.
Optional extension: Track C, Guard the Nation.

The Track A deliverable is a working demonstration on real satellite data. Track C is shown only after the Track A path works and remains protective: monitoring land and infrastructure, generating early warnings and evidence, and never tracking individual people.

2. Product statement

Drishti is a satellite-assisted audit layer for surface-visible public infrastructure. It compares reported status, spending and schedule with observable construction activity, then prioritizes locations for field verification.

Drishti does not accuse a contractor and does not claim that a satellite score is an exact completion percentage. Every alert means "verify on ground" and carries its evidence, confidence, observability and data-quality limits.

3. Judge-facing MVP

The 12-hour MVP is intentionally narrow:

- one real, surface-visible corridor;
- 10 to 20 one-kilometre segments;
- one known delayed or stalled case for chronological back-testing;
- Sentinel-2 plus Sentinel-1 where usable;
- precomputed imagery and features for demo reliability;
- one Satellite Activity Index;
- one transparent stall rule;
- one Activity-Reporting Divergence Score;
- one evidence-backed alert;
- one dashboard path that can be demonstrated in 90 seconds.

Two additional corridors, portfolio scaling and Track C are extensions, not dependencies of the MVP.

4. Core measurement contract

4.1 Satellite Activity Index

The Satellite Activity Index (SAI) is a relative score from 0 to 100. It measures observable surface activity against a segment's own pre-construction baseline. It is not an exact percentage of engineering completion.

Inputs may include:

- NDVI change for vegetation clearing;
- bare-soil and built-up indices;
- optical brightness or texture change;
- Sentinel-1 VV and VH backscatter change;
- linear-feature continuity;
- rainfall and valid-observation count as context.

The first implementation uses explicit, reviewable weights. A learned model may replace the weights only when sufficient labelled examples exist.

4.2 Activity-Reporting Divergence Score

The MVP replaces the invalid subtraction of reported completion percentage and a relative satellite index.

For each segment and date:

1. Convert the planned construction curve to an expected activity percentile.
2. Convert observed SAI history to an observed activity percentile.
3. Compute divergence = expected_activity_percentile - observed_activity_percentile.
4. Report the divergence with confidence, observability and observation count.

No completion percentage is inferred unless a separately trained and validated calibration model exists.

4.3 Stall rule

A segment becomes a stall candidate when:

- activity remains below its segment-specific threshold for three consecutive valid observations;
- rainfall or missing imagery does not explain the low activity;
- observability is medium or high.

The output state is active, slowing, stall_candidate, accelerating or not_assessable.

5. End-to-end data flow

PROJECT REGISTRY
  -> ALIGNMENT AND SEGMENTS
  -> SATELLITE OBSERVATIONS
  -> QUALITY MASKS AND PREPROCESSING
  -> SEGMENT FEATURES
  -> SATELLITE ACTIVITY INDEX
  -> STALL AND DIVERGENCE ANALYSIS
  -> DELAY-RISK BACK-TEST
  -> EVIDENCE PACKAGE
  -> ALERT API AND DASHBOARD

All derived objects retain source identifiers, processing version and timestamps.

6. Layer contracts

Layer 1: Project registry

Purpose: represent what the project officially claims.

Required fields:

- project_id and project name;
- sector and executing ministry or agency;
- sanctioned and latest cost;
- start, planned end and revised end dates;
- reported physical progress and spending;
- source URL and source-as-of date;
- data classification: real, synthetic or mixed.

Acceptance criteria:

- invalid percentages, negative money and impossible date order are rejected;
- duplicate project IDs are rejected;
- synthetic values can never be presented as real;
- derived cost and expenditure percentages are reproducible;
- normalized records export to JSON.

Layer 2: Alignment and segment builder

Input: authoritative alignment where available, otherwise verified OSM or Bhuvan geometry.
Output: buffered one-kilometre segments with stable IDs, baseline period and observability class.

Layer 3: Satellite observation ingestion

Input: corridor bounds, time range and sensor policy.
Output: a catalogue of source scenes and cached cloud-optimized imagery. Every observation retains scene ID, acquisition time, orbit or tile, licence and checksum.

Layer 4: Preprocessing and quality

Sentinel-2: surface-reflectance data, SCL cloud and shadow mask, corridor cloud fraction, and temporal composites.
Sentinel-1: prefer analysis-ready RTC for the hackathon. If raw GRD is used, apply calibration, terrain correction, speckle handling and co-registration.
Output: quality-controlled, co-registered observations plus valid-pixel counts.

Layer 5: Feature extraction

Output per segment and date: optical indices, SAR change, continuity, rainfall and quality metrics. Feature formulas and processing versions are stored with the results.

Layer 6: Satellite Activity Index

Normalize features against the same segment's baseline and combine them using explicit weights. Store the component contributions so judges can see why the score moved.

Layer 7: Stall and divergence analysis

Apply the stall rule and Activity-Reporting Divergence Score. Low-observability segments become not_assessable rather than guessed.

Layer 8: Delay-risk back-test

MVP: perform a chronological case-study back-test on one known delayed project. Use only observations that existed before the official delay notice. Report early-warning lead time and false alerts.

Future model: gradient boosting with project-level chronological splits, calibrated probabilities and feature explanations. Do not claim general accuracy from one case study.

Layer 9: Evidence package and alerts

Each alert contains segment ID, dates, before and after chips, SAI chart, divergence chart, rainfall note, data quality, observability, confidence, source scene IDs and the recommended action "field verification".

Layer 10: Dashboard

The judge path contains one map, one time slider, one before-and-after comparison, one planned/reported/observed chart and one evidence alert. A recorded offline fallback is kept for the pitch.

Layer 11: Optional Track C extension

Possible protective uses are right-of-way encroachment and illegal sand-mining indicators near infrastructure. Results remain alerts for authorized verification, not identification or tracking of people.

Layer 12: Business and deployment

Primary buyer for the pitch: a government project-monitoring or audit cell.
Decision changed: which segment receives field verification first.
Commercial model: annual per-project monitoring plus separately priced evidence reports.

Any market-size, project-value or savings figure must cite an official source. Savings are labelled illustrative until measured in a pilot. Buyer ROI is expressed as annual monitoring cost compared with the value of identifying one material delay or reporting discrepancy earlier.

7. Validation

Minimum MVP evidence:

- 20 to 30 hand-reviewed segment-date examples;
- one chronological delayed-project back-test;
- early-warning lead time;
- stall precision and recall where labels allow;
- number and share of not_assessable segments;
- sanity checks on completed and untouched segments;
- visible component contributions for the showcased alert.

8. Technology choices

- Python 3.11 or newer;
- GeoPandas, Rasterio, Xarray and STAC tooling for geospatial processing;
- GeoParquet initially, PostGIS when multi-user serving is required;
- scikit-learn or LightGBM for the later risk model;
- FastAPI for service endpoints;
- Streamlit for the hackathon dashboard;
- ReportLab or WeasyPrint for evidence exports.

9. Reliability and responsible use

- open or explicitly licensed data for the live demonstration;
- no individual tracking;
- no automatic allegation or enforcement decision;
- source provenance and processing version on every result;
- confidence lowered for clouds, missing observations and low observability;
- offline demo assets prepared before judging;
- secrets and service credentials kept outside the repository.

10. Twelve-hour build sequence

Hours 0 to 2: select and verify one corridor, load its project registry record and alignment.
Hours 2 to 4: retrieve or verify cached observations and quality masks.
Hours 4 to 6: compute segment features and the SAI.
Hours 6 to 7: run stall and divergence analysis.
Hours 7 to 8: complete the chronological back-test.
Hours 8 to 10: assemble the dashboard and one evidence alert.
Hours 10 to 11: verify economics, citations and limitations.
Hours 11 to 12: rehearse, time and record the fallback demo.

11. Five-minute pitch

0:00 to 0:30 - problem and decision Drishti improves.
0:30 to 2:00 - real-data live demonstration.
2:00 to 3:00 - transparent method and back-test result.
3:00 to 3:45 - buyer, price and illustrative value.
3:45 to 4:20 - limitations, confidence and responsible-use safeguards.
4:20 to 4:45 - optional Track C extension.
4:45 to 5:00 - conclusion and requested pilot.

12. Judging evidence checklist

Economic or strategic impact, 30 percent:
- one primary payer;
- one decision changed;
- sourced market or project value;
- buyer-level ROI example.

Technical depth and AI, 30 percent:
- real optical or SAR data;
- reproducible feature and SAI pipeline;
- explicit AI target and labels;
- chronological validation and explainability.

Feasibility, 20 percent:
- one corridor completed end to end;
- open data and a reproducible build;
- honest not_assessable state;
- demo runs offline.

Demo and pitch, 20 percent:
- visible before and after evidence;
- one map, curve and alert;
- measured back-test result;
- complete presentation in less than five minutes.
